# Chapter 2: The Right Sort

The scarlet engine whistled once, and King's Cross began to slide away. Harry watched the red-haired mother waving from the platform, and her small daughter running alongside, half laughing and half crying, until a curve lost them both. Houses flickered by, and Harry's heart wouldn't stop racing. He hadn't a clue what waited for him at the end of the line, but he was sure it was better than Privet Drive.

The compartment door rattled open. It was the youngest of the red-haired boys, the one whose mother had been scrubbing at his nose on the platform.

'Anyone sitting there?' he asked softly. 'Everywhere else is full.'

Harry shook his head. The boy sat down, stole a look at Harry's forehead, and then stared very hard out of the window as though he hadn't. There was still a smudge of soot on his nose.

Two identical faces appeared in the doorway.

'We're off down the train, Ron. Lee Jordan's got a tarantula!' the red-haired twins said in unison.

'Right,' mumbled Ron.

'Fred and George Weasley, by the way,' said one of the twins to Harry. 'He's Ron. Don't let him bore you.'

'Bye,' said Harry and Ron together, and the door slid shut.

'Are you really Harry Potter?' Ron asked barely a split second later.

Harry nodded.

'I thought they were winding me up! Have you actually got the, the...' Ron pointed vaguely towards Harry's forehead.

Harry lifted his fringe. Ron stared at the lightning scar.

'So that's where You-Know-Who...?'

'Yes,' said Harry. 'I don't remember anything, though.'

'Blimey,' breathed Ron, then, catching himself staring again, hastily looked back at the window.

'Is everyone in your family magical?' Harry blurted out after a while. Ron was just as interesting to him as he was to Ron.

'Er, yeah, I think so,' said Ron. 'Mum's got a second cousin who's an accountant, but nobody talks about him.'

'Then you must know heaps of magic already.'

The Weasleys were clearly another esteemed wizarding family, like the pale girl's in Diagon Alley.

'I heard something about you being brought up by Muggles,' said Ron. 'What are they like?'

'Terrible. Well, at least my aunt, uncle and cousin are. Would've traded anything for three wizard brothers.'

'Five,' said Ron, and his face dropped slightly. 'I'm the sixth of us to go to Hogwarts, my brothers have done just about everything. Bill was Head Boy, and Charlie captained the Quidditch team. Percy's just been made a prefect. Fred and George muck around, but their marks are good enough and they're likeable. So I'm expected to do as well as all of them, and if I do manage it nobody will care, because it's been done five times already.

'And you never get anything new with five brothers - I've got Bill's old robes and Charlie's old wand,' Ron said, 'and Percy's old rat.' He reached inside his jacket and pulled out a fat grey rat, sound asleep. 'This is Scabbers. He's hopeless, practically never wakes up. Dad got Percy an owl for being made prefect, but they couldn't aff... I mean I just got Scabbers.'

Ron's ears went scarlet. Perhaps thinking he had said too much, Ron turned again to the window.

Harry couldn't see anything shameful in not having money for an owl. A month ago he had never had a penny of his own and for all his life he'd been wearing his cousin Dudley's cast-off clothes. He told Ron as much, and Ron brightened considerably.

'...and I didn't know a thing about being a wizard until Hagrid showed up, or about my parents, or Voldemort...'

Ron gasped audibly.

'What?' asked Harry.

'You said his name!' Ron said, sounding horrified and amazed at the same time. 'I'd have thought you, of all people...'

'I didn't know you weren't supposed to,' said Harry. 'That's what I mean. I don't know anything.' It was out before he could stop it, the thing that had sat in his stomach since Hagrid told him he was a wizard. 'I'll probably be the worst in the class.'

'Nah,' said Ron. 'Loads of people come from Muggle families. Everyone picks it up quick enough.'

London had fallen away behind them as they became lost in conversation. For a time they watched fields and hedges go by, now dotted with sheep and cows. There was silence, but it was comfortable.

At twelve o'clock something came rattling down the corridor, and a plump, smiling lady opened the door.

'Fancy anything from the trolley, my loves?'

Harry, who had not had breakfast, jumped to his feet. Ron's ears went pink again and he muttered something about his mum's sandwiches.

The Dursleys had never given Harry any money for treats, and with his pockets full of golden Galleons and silver Sickles he was determined to buy every Mars Bar on the trolley. The only thing was, there wasn't a Mars Bar to be seen. Instead, there were Bertie Bott's Every-Flavour Beans and Drooble's Best Blowing Gum, Chocolate Frogs, Pumpkin Pasties, Cauldron Cakes and Liquorice Wands, and a great many other things Harry had never seen in his life. He couldn't bear to miss anything, so he bought some of each and handed over eleven silver Sickles and seven bronze Knuts. Ron's eyes grew big as Harry carried his prize and tipped it on the train bench.

'Peckish?'

'Famished,' said Harry, as he bit into a pumpkin pasty.

Ron had produced a lumpy parcel and was busy unwrapping it. He peeled four sandwiches from the parcel and sighed.

'Corned beef. Mum never remembers I can't stand it.'

'I'll swap you,' said Harry, holding out a pasty. 'Here.'

'You won't like it, it's dry as anything,' said Ron. 'She hasn't much time,' he added quickly. 'Not with the five of us.'

'Take it,' said Harry. He had never had anything to share before, and never anybody to share it with. It was a wonderful feeling, sitting with Ron and working steadily through the sweets and cakes, while the sandwiches lay forgotten next to them.

'What are these?' said Harry, picking up a packet of Chocolate Frogs. 'They can't be real frogs?' Very little would surprise him any more.

'Not really,' said Ron. 'Tell me which card you've got, though. I need Agrippa.'

'Card?'

'Oh, right, you wouldn't know. There's a card in every one, Famous Witches and Wizards, for collecting. I've got five hundred or so, but I can't get Agrippa or Ptolemy.'

Harry opened his frog and drew out the card. An old man looked up at him over half-moon spectacles. His nose was long and bent, and his hair and beard were silver and fell out of the picture altogether. The name beneath was Albus Dumbledore.

'So that's Dumbledore!' said Harry.

'You're not telling me you'd never heard of Dumbledore?' said Ron. 'Can I have one? I might get Agrippa. Cheers.'

Harry turned the card over.

*Albus Dumbledore, present Headmaster of Hogwarts. Widely held to be the greatest wizard of the age, Professor Dumbledore is best known for overcoming the Dark wizard Grindelwald in 1945, for discovering the twelve uses of dragon's blood, and for his alchemical work with his partner, Nicolas Flamel. The Professor is fond of chamber music and tenpin bowling.*

He turned it back, and nearly dropped it. The picture was empty.

'He's gone!'

'Well, he's not going to stand about all day, is he?' said Ron. 'He'll come back. Oh no, Morgana again, I've got six of her. D'you want it? You could start a collection.'

His gaze wandered to the pile of unopened frogs.

'Have as many as you like,' said Harry. 'Thing is, with Muggles, people don't move in photographs.'

'What? They never move?' Ron asked, astonished. 'That's mental.'

Harry watched Dumbledore stroll back into his frame and give him the smallest of smiles.

There was a knock, and a round-faced boy came in looking close to tears.

'Sorry,' he said. 'You haven't seen a toad?'

They shook their heads.

'I've lost him!' the boy wailed. 'He's always escaping!'

'He'll turn up,' said Harry.

'Yes,' said the boy miserably. 'Well, if you do see him...'

He went out.

'Can't think why he minds,' said Ron. 'If I had a toad I'd lose it on purpose. Still, I brought Scabbers, so I'm one to talk.'

The rat slept on in his lap.

'He could be dead and you'd never tell,' said Ron with disgust. 'I had a go at turning him yellow yesterday to liven him up. Didn't work. Here, I'll show you...'

He dug about in his trunk and came up with a wand that had seen better days. It was chipped along its length, and something white gleamed at the tip.

'The unicorn hair's nearly coming out. Anyway...'

He had only just raised it when the door opened again. The boy who had lost his toad was back, and he had brought a girl with him, already dressed in her Hogwarts robes.

'Has anybody seen a toad? Neville's lost one,' she said. Her voice was brisk and rather commanding, her brown hair stuck out in a great thick bush, and her front teeth were a little too big for her.

'We told him already, we haven't,' said Ron, but she was looking at his wand.

'Oh, are you doing magic? Go on, then, let's see.'

She sat down. Ron looked rather startled.

'Er. All right.'

He cleared his throat.

'Buttercup, custard, morning sun, make this lazy rat a yellow one.'

He flourished the wand. Scabbers remained grey and went on sleeping.

'Are you certain that's a proper spell?' said the girl. 'It isn't a very good one, is it? I've tried a few easy ones for practice and they all worked. There's no magic in my family whatsoever, so the letter was a tremendous surprise, but I was so pleased, because it's the best school of witchcraft anywhere, everyone says so. I've memorised all our set books, naturally, and I only hope that's enough. I'm Hermione Granger. Who are you?'

It all came out in a single breath.

Harry glanced at Ron and was comforted to see from his dazed expression that he hadn't memorised the set books either.

'Ron Weasley,' Ron muttered.

'Harry Potter,' said Harry.

'Are you actually?' said Hermione. 'I know all about you, of course. I bought some extra books for background reading, and you're in *Modern Magical History*, and *The Rise and Fall of the Dark Arts*, and *Great Wizarding Events of the Twentieth Century*.'

'Am I?' said Harry faintly.

'Goodness, you didn't know? I'd have looked up every word if it were me,' said Hermione. 'Do you two know which house you'll be in? I've been asking people, and I'm hoping for Gryffindor. It sounds much the best, and they say Dumbledore was in it himself. I expect Ravenclaw would be all right too. Well, we ought to go and find Neville's toad. And you should both get changed, I should think we'll be arriving before long.'

She swept out, and the toadless boy trailed after her.

'I don't care which house I get, as long as she's not in it,' said Ron, flinging his wand back into the trunk. 'Rubbish spell. George gave it to me. I bet he knew it was a dud.'

Harry was about to answer when something pale flashed past the glass of the compartment door. It was a curtain of white-blonde hair, moving briskly down the corridor. Then it came back.

The door opened, and in walked the girl from Madam Malkin's. She was already in her robes, which fell immaculately around. There was a ribbon of dark green silk in her hair, matching her unsettling eyes that found Harry immediately.

'You might have said... that you were Harry Potter,' she said sternly, looking only at Harry.

Harry stared at her. This was some way to start a conversation.

'You didn't ask,' he said.

Something flickered in those green eyes and was gone.

'Well, I do hope you remember my name. Dracina Malfoy.'

Ron coughed. It sounded very much like a snigger.

Dracina Malfoy rounded on him.

'Something amusing?' Her voice cut like a knife.

'It's clear you're a Weasley, thanks to Father you're easy to spot.' She turned back to Harry.

'The most valuable thing you should know is that not all wizarding families are created equal. You may want to think about your future and surround yourself with the right people.'

Harry was just now realising there were two others behind Dracina. One was a tall dark-haired boy with broad shoulders, with a face that almost could have belonged in an oil painting. The other was a girl with a long dark plait and a sharp, clever face.

It was another second before Harry had realised how quiet the cabin had become, in fact how quiet the whole train now seemed.

'I reckon I can figure that out for myself, thanks,' Harry finally said, coolly.

There was a moment where the silence continued and nothing in Dracina's face moved at all. Then that familiar pink colour came into her cheeks and Harry felt a satisfied feeling growing in his chest. The boy and girl behind her were trying to hide their shock unsuccessfully.

'I would watch my step if I were you, Potter,' Malfoy said slowly.

'You don't want to end up like your parents.'

Harry and Ron found themselves both on their feet, but Malfoy's bodyguards had immediately stepped forward just as she turned her back and was already walking away.

'Crabbe, Goyle, let's go,' she said over her shoulder, disappearing down the train corridor.

Crabbe and Goyle backed out after her without a word, and neither of them took their eyes off Harry until the last moment. Then one of them slid the door shut, gently.

When the door had closed, Harry and Ron looked at each other. Both of their faces red, and Ron's ears were practically glowing.

'Blimey, Malfoys... Dad warned me about them. Some of the first to come back over to our side when You-Know-Who vanished. Claimed they'd been bewitched. Dad says they never needed an excuse to go over to the other side. But you've already met her?'

Harry told him about Madam Malkin's and embarrassingly how he'd thought she came from some wizard aristocracy. Ron laughed so hard that it wasn't long before they both were feeling better about the whole situation.

A moment later, Hermione Granger was in the doorway.

'What on earth has been happening?' she said. 'Someone said there'd been an argument. You can't already be in trouble before we've even arrived! Also, you need to get your robes on quickly, I've been up to the front to ask the driver, and he says we're almost there.'

'Well, d'you mind giving us a chance to change?' Ron said bluntly.

'Fine. I only came in because everybody out here is being so childish, tearing up and down the corridor,' Hermione said with a sniff. 'Oh, and there's dirt on your nose, in case you didn't know,' she told Ron as she closed the door, and he glowered at it.

Harry glanced out of the window. The light was fading. Mountains and forests stood dark beneath a deep purple sky, and the train seemed to be slowing.

'Praying she doesn't end up in the same house as us,' said Ron.

Harry realised he could be speaking about Hermione or Malfoy, but before he could ask, the students in their black robes were bustling through the corridor  the train.
