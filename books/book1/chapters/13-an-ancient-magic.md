# Chapter 13: An Ancient Magic

It was Quirrell.

‘You!’ gasped Harry. Beside him, Dracina froze.

Quirrell was standing in the middle of the chamber with his back to the door, quite still, and there was no sign of the twitching, flinching, apologetic man who’d spent the whole year jumping at his own shadow. His hands were folded behind him. When he turned, his face was perfectly calm, and that was somehow worse than anything.

Quirrell smiled cruelly.

‘Me,’ he said. ‘I did wonder whether it’d be you I met down here, Potter.’ His eyes slid to Dracina. ‘Though not in such company.’

‘But I thought... Snape...’ Harry trailed off.

Quirrell laughed low and dry and cold. ‘Not Severus, no. It’s been wonderfully convenient, having him sweep about the place like a great bat in a bad mood. Stand him next to me, and who’d ever look twice at p-p-poor, st-stammering P-Professor Quirrell?’

Things were starting to become horribly clear for Harry. The stranger in the hood in the village, who’d wanted to know all about Fluffy. But there was one thing that didn’t fit.

‘Snape tried to knock me off my broom,’ he said. ‘He wanted me dead.’

‘No, you foolish boy. _I_ wanted you dead! Your rather obsessive friend Miss Granger accidentally knocked me over as she rushed to set fire to Snape at that Quidditch match. She broke my eye contact with you. Another few seconds and you’d have been on the ground in pieces. I’d have had you if Severus hadn’t been sitting there mumbling a counter-curse under his breath, trying to save you.’

‘Save me?’

‘Yes. Why d’you suppose he wanted to referee the next match? To make sure I didn’t have another go. He needn’t have troubled himself. I couldn’t do a thing with Dumbledore sitting in the stands. And all for nothing, since tonight I will kill you anyway.’

Quirrell turned away from Harry. ‘It’s funny, Potter. For all I knew you’d seen me coming to look at what was guarding the Stone on Hallowe’en.’

‘You let the troll in...’ Dracina said quietly.

‘Naturally, girl. Trolls and I have an understanding. While the rest of them were running round the castle hunting for it, Severus, who’d had his eye on me from the start, went straight up to the third floor to cut me off. Pity that my troll didn’t manage to beat you to death and that that wretched dog didn’t take Severus’s leg off properly.’ He drew a breath. ‘Now silence, the both of you. I need to examine this mirror.’

‘It’s the Mirror...’ Dracina whispered as she and Harry realised what Quirrell was standing in front of.

The Mirror of Erised. The gold frame, the clawed feet. Quirrell was gazing into it with a hunger that made his face look gaunt.

Beside him, Dracina’s eyes darted away from the glass, to him.

‘This mirror is the key to finding the Stone,’ Quirrell said. He gave the frame a few experimental taps with his wand, then with his knuckles, as though it might be hollow. ‘Of course, trust Dumbledore to come up with something so cryptic.’

All Harry could think of doing was to keep Quirrell talking. Every second the man spent answering him was a second he wasn’t giving to the Mirror.

‘You and Snape, in the Forest -’ he blurted out.

‘Yes,’ Quirrell answered absent-mindedly. ‘Snape suspected me all along, tried to frighten me. But my fear is reserved only for my Lord...’

Quirrell went round the back of the Mirror, and came out again, his composure beginning to slip.

‘I see it,’ he growled. ‘I see the Stone. I’m holding it up, Master. But where *is* it?’

‘But Snape hates me...’ Harry interrupted. 

‘Oh, he does,’ said Quirrell, without looking around. ‘Make no mistake about that. He was at school with your father, didn’t you know? They detested each other. But he never wanted you dead. Now quiet, I must concentrate.’

Quirrell paused. ‘I don’t understand it. Is the Stone *inside* the glass? Should I break it?’

Harry’s mind was racing now.

‘What does it do? How does it work? Help me, Master!’ Quirrell shouted.

And then a voice answered. It was high and thin and cold. It came from Quirrell himself.

‘[Use the boy... use the boy...]{.smallcaps}’

Quirrell wheeled round. Dracina and Harry both flinched back together.

‘Tell me what you see, Potter!’

Harry’s heart felt like it would burst from his chest. He shut his eyes, then opened them and looked into the Mirror.

There he was. Pale and frightened. Then the reflection smiled.

It put its hand in its pocket, drew out a stone the colour of blood, winked at him and put the Stone back in its pocket. And as it did, Harry felt something heavy drop into his own real pocket, against his leg. His eyes went to his pocket.

Somehow, impossibly, he had the Stone.

He realised Dracina’s eyes were also on his pocket. Then they were on him. Harry gave the smallest shake of his head. Dracina’s eyes grew big and flickered wildly.

‘Well?’ said Quirrell, without turning from the glass. ‘What do you see?’

Harry screwed his courage together and said the first lie that came to his head.

‘I see myself shaking hands with Dumbledore, winning the House Cup.’

‘[He lies... he lies...]{.smallcaps}’

‘The truth, Potter!’ Quirrell shouted.

‘[Let me speak to him... face to face...]{.smallcaps}’

‘Master, you’re not strong enough!’

‘[I have strength enough... for this...]{.smallcaps}’

Harry felt as though Devil’s Snare had come up through the floor and taken him by the ankles. He could only stand and watch as Quirrell reached up and started to unwind his turban.

The cloth came away in loops. Harry would have screamed, if he’d had any sound left in him. Next to him, Dracina gasped and grabbed his arm.

On the back of Quirrell’s head, pushing out of the skull like a parasite, was the most terrible thing Harry had ever seen. It was grey-white and stretched thin over the bone, and where a nose should have been there were two slits, like a snake’s.

‘[Harrrry Potttter...]{.smallcaps}’ it whispered.

Harry felt Dracina’s grip tighten on his arm. His entire body felt frozen.

‘[Seeee what I’ve become?]{.smallcaps}’ said the face. ‘[A shadow. I have a shape only when I can borrow another’s... but there have always been those willing to let me in... Unicorn blood has strengthened me these past weeks... and once I have the Elixir of Life, I shall make a body of my own... Join me... or you’ll meet the same fate as your parents... They died begging me for mercy...]{.smallcaps}’

Feeling came back into Harry’s body all at once.

‘LIAR!’ Harry shouted.

‘[How touching,]{.smallcaps}’ it hissed. ‘[I always did value courage... Yes, boy, your parents were brave... I killed your father first, and he fought me well... but your mother needn’t have died... she was trying to protect you...]{.smallcaps}’

Tears welled up in Harry’s eyes, and a burning sensation was rising inside him.

‘[Harry... There is no good and evil. There is only power and those too weak to reach for it... Or perhaps... Yes... The girl may understand...]{.smallcaps}’

Harry’s eyes shot to Dracina. She held his gaze briefly before her eyes flashed towards his pocket.

Harry’s stomach dropped.

When Dracina’s gaze came back to Harry, he could see her hesitation in them. She was actually thinking about it. Of course she was. 

‘Don’t,’ Harry said.

Dracina fought desperately to hold back tears.

‘No...’ Harry begged, his voice rising.

‘I’m so scared,’ she pleaded back, ‘I wouldn’t...’

‘Of course you would!’ Harry screamed. The heat inside him had reached boiling point. He rounded on Dracina.

‘HE TOOK EVERYTHING FROM ME!’ Harry heard himself roaring.

‘Harry!’ Dracina wailed as she backed away, one hand reaching behind her for a wall that wasn’t there.

‘AND YOU WOULD JUST GIVE IT TO HIM?’

‘[He has the Stone! Seize him!]{.smallcaps}’ shrieked Voldemort.

Harry had already lunged for Dracina, but Quirrell got there first. His hand closed on Harry’s wrist, and Harry seized his in return. Searing pain exploded from Harry’s scar all the way through his body. There was nothing but agony.

‘Aaa[aaa]{.smallcaps}aa[gh!]{.smallcaps}’

Quirrell and Voldemort’s howl echoed throughout the chamber.

‘Master, I can’t hold him! My hands! My *hands!*’

Quirrell was stumbling backwards, holding hands that had melted where Harry touched them.

‘[Kill him!]{.smallcaps}’ screeched Voldemort.

At once Harry threw himself at Quirrell, vision blurred and pain somehow multiplying. He raised his hands to Quirrell’s face.

Harry’s senses were giving up. He didn’t hear Quirrell make a sound, but he felt flesh evaporating beneath his hands. His vision was molten white. Dimly he felt someone pulling him back and somewhere far off, he heard a voice. He could have sworn it was his mother’s.

Everything started to fade to black, but not before he was left with one last flash of green eyes.

---

Something gold was glinting above him.

The Snitch! Harry reached for it, but he couldn’t move his arms. He blinked. It wasn’t the Snitch. It was a pair of half-moon spectacles. How odd. 

He blinked again, and the face of Albus Dumbledore swam into place above him, smiling.

‘Good afternoon, Harry,’ said Dumbledore.

Harry stared at him. Then it all came back in a rush.

‘Professor... Professor! The Stone! He has it! Professor, quick...’

‘Calm yourself, dear boy. You’re a little behind the times,’ said Dumbledore. ‘Quirrell does not have the Stone.’

Harry tried to throw himself out of bed. He got about two inches. Dumbledore’s hand was flat on his chest, holding him down with a strength that didn’t belong to an old man at all.

‘Then who does? Sir, I...’

‘Harry, please lie still, or Madam Pomfrey will have me thrown out, and I should like to finish our conversation.’

Harry swallowed and looked about him. He was in the hospital wing. The sheets were white and smelled of starch, and on the table beside the bed was what looked like the entire stock of a sweet-shop.

‘Tokens from your friends and admirers,’ said Dumbledore, beaming at the pile. ‘What passed between you and Professor Quirrell in the dungeons is, of course, a complete secret, and so, naturally, the entire school knows every word of it. I believe your friends Messrs Fred and George Weasley attempted to send you a lavatory seat. No doubt they felt it would cheer you up. Madam Pomfrey took the view that it was unhygienic, and confiscated it.’

‘How long have I been here?’

‘Three days. Mr Ronald Weasley and Miss Granger will be very glad you’ve come round. They’ve been beside themselves.’

‘But sir, the Stone...’

‘I see you won’t be put off. Very well. The Stone. Professor Quirrell didn’t manage to take it from you.’

‘You found us? You got Hermione’s owl?’

‘No, Harry. I’d no sooner landed in London than I understood that the one place I ought to be was the one I’d just left. I came back as fast as I could.’

‘So it was you. You saved me.’

Dumbledore didn’t answer at once.

‘I was too late, Harry,’ he said. ‘Your efforts should have killed you. By the time I reached the chamber, Professor Quirrell was dead, and Voldemort had fled.’

He looked at Harry over his half-moon spectacles for a long moment. ‘You were not alone when I found you. Miss Malfoy...’ He watched Harry’s face closely at the name and seemed to decide something. He folded his hands. ‘As for the Stone, it has been destroyed.’

‘Destroyed?’ said Harry blankly. ‘But Nicolas Flamel...’

‘Oh, so you know about Nicolas?’ said Dumbledore, and he looked delighted. ‘You really did this thoroughly, didn’t you? Well, Nicolas and I have had a little talk, and we’ve agreed that it’s all for the best.’

‘But that means he’ll die, won’t he? Him and his wife?’

‘They have enough Elixir put by to set their affairs in order. And then, yes, they will die.’

He smiled at the look on Harry’s face.

‘To someone as young as you, I know it sounds like madness. But to Nicolas and Perenelle it really is rather like going to bed after a very, very long day. To the well-organised mind, Harry, death is only the next great adventure. The Stone was never such a wonderful thing, you know,’ Dumbledore went on. ‘All the money and all the life you could want. The two things most people would pick over anything else and the trouble is, people have a knack for picking exactly what’s worst for them.’

Harry lay there in silence. Dumbledore hummed a small tune while smiling at the ceiling.

‘Sir?’ said Harry at last. ‘I’ve been thinking. Even if the Stone’s gone... Vol... I mean, You-Know-Who...’

‘Call him Voldemort, Harry. Always use the proper name for a thing. Being afraid of a name only makes you more afraid of the thing itself.’

‘Yes, sir. Well. Voldemort’s going to try other ways of coming back, isn’t he? I mean, he’s not *gone*, is he?’

‘No, Harry, he is not. He’s out there somewhere still, looking for another body to share, perhaps. Not being properly alive, he can’t be killed.’

Harry nodded, and stopped at once, because it made his head ring like a bell.

‘Sir, respectfully,’ he said. ‘I’d like to know the truth about a great many things.’

‘The truth.’ Dumbledore sighed. ‘It’s a beautiful and terrible thing, the truth, and it ought to be handled with care. But I’ll answer your questions, unless I’ve a very good reason not to, and in that case I’ll ask you to forgive me. I shan’t lie to you. That I promise.’

‘Well,’ said Harry. ‘Voldemort said he only killed my mother because she tried to stop him killing me. But why did he want to kill *me* in the first place?’

Dumbledore paused for a moment.

‘Alas, the first question you ask me, I cannot answer. Not today. One day you will know, and when you are ready I will tell you everything. Is that acceptable to you, Harry?’

Though he’d have accepted anything from Dumbledore just then, Harry knew not to argue.

‘But I can tell you this. Your mother died to save you. If there is one thing Voldemort has never understood, it is love. It is an ancient magic, Harry, older than any of us, and it needs no wand.’

‘How?’ asked Harry.

‘Though your mother and father may be gone, their life, and their power, and most importantly their love, live on within you, Harry. There’s no scar for it. Nothing you could see. But to have been loved that deeply, even once the person who loved us has gone, leaves a kind of protection on us for good. It’s in your very skin. Quirrell shared his soul with Voldemort and couldn’t lay a hand on you for that reason. It was impossible to touch a person marked by something so good.’

Harry couldn’t prevent the tears that sprang to his eyes. Dumbledore looked out of the window, where a pair of jackdaws were squabbling on the sill, and seemed to find them very interesting.

When he’d found his voice again, Harry said, ‘And the Invisibility Cloak. Do you know who gave it me?’

‘Ah. Your father happened to leave it with me, and I thought you might like to have it.’ Dumbledore’s eyes twinkled. ‘Useful things, Cloaks. Your father used his mainly for sneaking down to the kitchens for food, as I recall.’

‘And there’s something else.’

‘Fire away.’

‘Quirrell said Snape...’

‘_Professor_ Snape, Harry.’

‘Yes. Him. Quirrell said he hates me because he hated my dad. Is that true?’

‘Your father did do something that Professor Snape has never been able to forgive.’

‘What?’

‘He saved his life.’

‘*What?*’

‘Yes,’ said Dumbledore dreamily. ‘Funny, isn’t it, how people’s minds work? Professor Snape couldn’t bear to be in your father’s debt. I rather think he worked so hard to keep you alive this year because it would make him and your father even. Then he could go back to hating your father’s memory in peace.’

Harry tried to make sense of it, and it made his head pound, so he gave up.

‘And sir. There’s one more thing.’

‘Only one?’

‘How did I get the Stone out of the Mirror?’

‘Ah, now, I’m glad you asked. It was one of my better ideas, and between you and me, that’s saying something. You see, only somebody who wanted to *find* the Stone, find it but not use it, would be able to get it out. Anyone else would just see themselves turning out gold, or drinking the Elixir. I do surprise myself, sometimes.’ He clapped his hands together. ‘Now, no more questions. I suggest you make a start on these sweets. Ah! Bertie Bott’s Every-Flavour Beans! I had the misfortune in my youth to come across one that tasted of vomit, and I’m afraid I’ve never quite trusted them since. But I think I’ll be safe with a nice toffee, don’t you?’

He smiled, picked a golden-brown bean out of the box, and popped it in his mouth. Then he choked.

‘Alas!’ he said. ‘Earwax!’

---

Madam Pomfrey, the matron, was a kind woman, but she ran the hospital wing like a fortress.

‘Just five minutes,’ Harry pleaded.

‘Absolutely not.’

‘You let Professor Dumbledore in.’

‘That was the Headmaster. That’s entirely different. You need rest.’

‘I *am* resting. Look. Lying down and everything. Oh, go on, Madam Pomfrey...’

‘Oh, very well,’ she said. ‘But five minutes. Not a second more.’

And she let Ron and Hermione in.

‘*Harry!*’

Hermione looked ready to throw herself across the bed, but Harry was glad she held herself back because his head was still quite sore.

‘Oh, Harry, we thought you were going to... Dumbledore was so worried...’

‘The whole school’s talking about it,’ said Ron. ‘Not about Malfoy, mind. Nobody seems to know she was down there. I think she’s sworn revenge on all of us for dragging her into this, as if we could have done anything to stop her! What really happened at the end?’

It was one of those rare times when the real story was stranger than anything the rumours had come up with. Harry told them everything. Quirrell, the Mirror, the Stone.

Ron and Hermione were a great audience. They gasped in the right places, and when Harry told them what was under Quirrell’s turban, Hermione shrieked out loud.

‘So the Stone’s gone?’ Ron said at last. ‘Flamel’s just going to *die*?’

‘That’s what I said. But Dumbledore reckons... what was it... “to the well-organised mind, death is only the next great adventure”.’

‘I always said he needed his racquet restrung,’ said Ron, looking rather impressed at how mad his hero was.

‘So what happened to you two?’ said Harry.

‘Well, I got back all right,’ said Hermione. ‘And I brought Ron round, which took a while...’

‘She slapped me,’ said Ron.

‘I did *not*, I patted you...’

‘She slapped me. Twice.’

‘...and we were running up to the Owlery to get a message to Dumbledore when we met him in the Entrance Hall. He already knew. He said, “Harry’s gone after him, hasn’t he?” and went tearing off to the third floor.’

‘D’you reckon he *meant* you to do it?’ said Ron. ‘Sending you your dad’s Cloak and all that?’

‘Well,’ Hermione rattled off, ‘if he *did*, I mean to say, that’s *terrible*, you could have been *killed*...’

‘No, it isn’t,’ said Harry slowly. ‘He’s a great man, Dumbledore. I think he knows more or less everything that goes on in this place, you know. I reckon he taught us enough to help in case we did end up there. I don’t think it was an accident he let me find out how the Mirror worked.’

The three of them sat in a comfortable silence for a while.

‘Listen, Harry,' Ron said eventually. 'You’ve got to be up for the end-of-year feast tomorrow. The points are all in and Slytherin won, of course. You missed the last Quidditch match. We were smashed by Ravenclaw without you. But the food’ll be good.’

Right then Madam Pomfrey came bustling down the ward.

‘You’ve had nearly fifteen minutes. Now OUT,’ she said sternly.

---

After a good night’s sleep Harry felt almost himself again.

‘I want to go to the feast,’ he told Madam Pomfrey, as she straightened the towers of sweet boxes on his side-table. ‘I can, can’t I?’

‘Professor Dumbledore says you’re to be allowed,’ she said, with a sniff, as though Professor Dumbledore had no idea how dangerous a feast could be. ‘And you’ve another visitor.’

‘Oh, good,’ said Harry. ‘Who?’

Hagrid came sideways through the door as he spoke. As usual when he was indoors, Hagrid looked too big to be allowed. He sat down on the chair by the bed, took one look at Harry and burst into tears.

‘It’s all my ruddy fault!’ he sobbed into his hands. ‘I told the evil git how ter get past Fluffy! I told him! It was the one thing he didn’ know an’ I *told* him! Yeh could’ve died! All fer a dragon egg! I’ll never touch a drop o’ drink again! I should be chucked out an’ made ter live as a Muggle!’

‘Hagrid!’ said Harry. He’d never seen Hagrid like this, shaking all over, great tears running down into his beard. ‘Hagrid, he’d have found out somehow. He’d have got it out of someone even if you’d never said a word.’

‘Yeh could’ve *died!*’ sobbed Hagrid. ‘An’ don’ say the name!’

‘VOLDEMORT!’ Harry bellowed, and Hagrid was so startled that he stopped crying. ‘I’ve met him, and I’m calling him by his name. Cheer up, Hagrid. We saved the Stone. It’s gone, he can’t use it. Have a Chocolate Frog, I’ve got loads...’

Hagrid wiped his nose on the back of his hand.

‘That reminds me,’ he said thickly. ‘I’ve got yeh a present.’

‘It’s not a stoat sandwich, is it?’ said Harry nervously, and at last Hagrid gave a weak chuckle.

‘Nah. Dumbledore gave me the day off yesterday ter sort it out. ’Course, he shoulda sacked me instead... anyway. Got yeh this.’

It was a book, bound in leather. Harry opened it.

It was full of wizard photographs. On every page, smiling and waving up at him, were his mother and father. One taken by a lake. One where they were in a garden, with his father’s arm round his mother and his mother laughing at something out of the picture. One with his mother holding a baby with black hair who kept trying to grab her nose.

‘Sent owls off ter all yer mum an’ dad’s old school friends, askin’ fer photos,’ said Hagrid. ‘Knew yeh didn’ have any. D’yeh like it?’

Harry couldn’t speak. Hagrid understood that well enough.

---

Harry went down to the end-of-year feast on his own that night. Madam Pomfrey had kept him back for one last check-up and a quick dose of something that tasted of pepper, so by the time he got to the Great Hall the whole school was in it. The Hall was done out in green and silver for Slytherin’s seventh House Cup in a row. An enormous banner hung behind the High Table with the Slytherin serpent on it, and the Slytherin table were looking very happy with themselves.

As Harry walked in there was a sudden hush, and then everybody started talking at once, loudly. He slid into a seat between Ron and Hermione and did his best not to notice that people were standing up to get a look at him. Luckily Dumbledore came in a moment later, and the noise died away.

‘Another year gone!’ Dumbledore said cheerfully. ‘And before we fall on this excellent feast, I’m afraid you must put up with an old man’s waffling for a minute or two. What a year it’s been! I hope your heads are all a little fuller than they were in September. You’ve the whole summer to get them nice and empty again before next year.

‘Now. The House Cup wants awarding, and the points stand thus: in fourth place, Gryffindor at three hundred and twelve points. In third, Hufflepuff, with three hundred and fifty-two. Ravenclaw have four hundred and twenty-six and Slytherin, four hundred and seventy-two.’

The Slytherin table erupted with cheers and roars. Harry spotted Malfoy clapping slowly with the rest of the table.

‘Yes, yes, well done Slytherin,’ said Dumbledore. ‘However, recent events have to be taken into account.’

The room went silent.

‘Ahem,’ said Dumbledore. ‘I have a few last-minute points to give out. Let me see. Yes.

‘First. To Mr Ronald Weasley...’

Ron went purple. He looked like a radish left out in the sun.

‘...for the best-played game of chess this school has seen in many years, I award Gryffindor house fifty points.’

The Gryffindor cheers nearly raised the ceiling, the stars overhead seemed startled. Percy could be heard telling the other prefects, ‘That’s my brother, you know! My youngest brother! Got past McGonagall’s chess set!’

Eventually there was quiet again.

‘Second. To Miss Hermione Granger. For keeping a cool head when others were in grave danger, I award Gryffindor house fifty points.’

Hermione buried her face in her arms. Harry strongly suspected she was crying. All along the table Gryffindors were beside themselves. They were a hundred points up.

‘Third. To Mr Harry Potter,’ said Dumbledore, and the Hall went deadly quiet. ‘For pure nerve and outstanding bravery, I award Gryffindor house sixty points.’

The din was deafening. Those who could add up were yelling themselves hoarse because they knew now that Gryffindor had four hundred and seventy-two points, tied with Slytherin. They would share the House Cup. If only Dumbledore had given Harry one more point.

Dumbledore raised a hand, and the Hall settled, slowly.

‘There are many kinds of courage,’ he said, smiling. ‘It takes a great deal of courage to stand up to our foes, but even more to stand up to our friends. I therefore award ten points to Mr Neville Longbottom.’

Someone floating in the middle of the Black Lake might have thought an explosion had gone off in Hogwarts, such was the roar from the Great Hall. Harry, Ron and Hermione tackled Neville, still white with shock, and he vanished beneath half the Gryffindor table. Harry looked up and over to the Slytherin table and saw that Malfoy had her head in her hands. The thought crossed his mind that she had been down there with them.

‘Which means,’ Dumbledore called over the applause, ‘we need a small change of decoration.’

He clapped his hands. In an instant the green hangings turned scarlet and the silver turned gold. The great serpent vanished, and a towering Gryffindor lion stood in its place. Snape was shaking Professor McGonagall’s hand with a dreadful fixed smile. His eye met Harry’s, and Harry knew at once that nothing Snape felt about him had changed a single hair.

Harry didn’t mind. Next year would be a normal, uneventful year at Hogwarts.

It was the best moment of Harry’s life. Better than winning at Quidditch, better than Christmas or even knocking out mountain trolls. Harry would never forget it, as long as he lived.

---

Harry had almost forgotten about the exam results, but they came all the same. Harry and Ron passed with good marks. Hermione, of course, came top of the year.

‘A hundred and twelve on Charms,’ said Ron, reading over her shoulder. ‘How is that even *possible*? What did you do, answer the questions he didn’t ask?’

‘Flitwick sets a bonus question,’ said Hermione, pink with pleasure. ‘Nobody ever reads to the end of the paper.’

Even Neville scraped through, his Herbology mark making up for his terrible Potions mark. And suddenly their wardrobes were empty and their trunks were full. Neville’s toad was found lurking in a corner of the boys’ toilets. Notes went round warning everyone not to use magic over the holidays.

‘I always hope they’ll forget to hand these out,’ said Fred Weasley sadly.

Hagrid took them down to the fleet of little boats that sailed across the lake, and then they were on the Hogwarts Express, talking and laughing as the countryside outside went greener and tidier, and eating Bertie Bott’s Every-Flavour Beans as the train tore past Muggle towns.

‘Grass,’ said Ron, chewing. ‘That’s definitely grass.’

‘Give me one,’ said Hermione. She took a green one, considered it, and put it in her mouth, and her face went very still. ‘Sprouts,’ she said.

Harry laughed, and for a while, in the hot compartment with the sun coming in and Ron’s feet up on the seat, he forgot where the train was going.

‘Feels odd, going home,’ said Hermione.

‘I’m not going home,’ said Harry. ‘Not really.’
