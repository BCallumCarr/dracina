# Chapter 1: A Chance Encounter

For ten years Harry had slept in a cupboard.

The Dursleys had treated him as something to be shut away. They had never tolerated the extraordinary things that seemed to happen around him, and that had turned into pure disdain when he received his invitation to Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry. Now he had never felt more at home standing in the wizarding world, in Diagon Alley outside Madam Malkin's Robes for All Occasions with pockets full of gold wizard Galleons. Hagrid, the small giant who had rescued Harry from the Dursleys on his eleventh birthday, had just ducked off to the Leaky Cauldron and left Harry to buy his new school robes. Witches passed him in colours he had no names for. A boy went by in a cloak with silver fastenings. He stepped into the shop and a bell tinkled.

The shop smelled of new cloth and hot irons. Bolts of black fabric rose to the ceiling.

'Hogwarts, dear?' said a plump, cheerful witch with a mouthful of pins and a tape measure draped round her neck, before Harry had managed a word. 'Pop up on here. I've just finished with this young lady.'

Harry walked up to stand next to the most striking girl he had ever seen. She had a pale, pointed face, and hair so fair it was nearly white, falling immaculately straight to her waist. When she turned to look at him, he saw that her eyes were almost luminous green.

She studied him for a moment, head tilted sideways. Then she did a deep curtsy, in the manner of someone who practised daily in front of a mirror.

'Dracina Malfoy,' she said grandly.

Harry just stared at her. Madam Malkin dropped a robe over his head and set to work.

'One bows...' she said, seeing from the corner of her eye that Harry had frozen.

'Uh.' Harry managed a stiff, unnatural bow, which earned him a tsk from Madam Malkin. They both straightened up. Then, because it was the only explanation he could think of, he asked seriously, 'Are you some kind of royalty?'

For just a second Dracina's mouth fell open and high on her pale cheeks two spots of pink appeared.

'I come from an esteemed family,' she said finally, 'but no, I am not a princess.'

'Sorry,' said Harry. 'This is all very new to me.'

Dracina looked at him as though he'd told her he'd never been taught to breathe.

'Curious,' she said softly.

Dracina seemed to decide that it was time to change the subject. She had a firm, arresting way of speaking, each word placed rather than offered.

'My father is next door with the bookseller. My mother is seeing about a wand.' She glanced towards the window, then back to Harry. 'And then I plan to convince them to buy me a racing broom -- terrible rule, don't you think, first-years not being allowed to bring their own to Hogwarts? Have you got a broom yet?' she asked, testing.

'No,' said Harry.

'But you do play Quidditch?'

'No,' said Harry again. He hadn't the faintest idea what Quidditch was.

'So I suppose you won't be playing at Hogwarts. You'll most probably be buried in books in house Ravenclaw, or potting mandrakes as a Hufflepuff.'

'Uh, no,' said Harry, confused. He was beginning to feel that it was the only word he knew.

'Nobody can be certain, of course, but I expect I shall be in Slytherin. My entire family has been. I'd probably go straight home if I were placed in Hufflepuff.'

'Mmm,' said Harry. He wished very much that he could think of something clever to say.

'Look! What _is_ that?' said Dracina, pointing at the front window. Hagrid was standing on the other side of the glass, beaming at Harry and holding up two enormous ice-creams by way of explaining why he was staying outside.

'That's Hagrid,' said Harry, delighted to know something she didn't. 'He works at Hogwarts.'

'Oh, I remember now,' said Dracina. 'He's a servant of sorts.'

'He's the gamekeeper,' said Harry. Harry was getting increasingly annoyed that he'd implied this girl was royalty.

'That's what I said. Father told me he's practically wild. He lives in a shack, and when he tries to use any sort of magic he sets his bed alight.'

'I think he's brilliant,' said Harry, his voice coming out cold.

'Do you really?' Dracina tilted her head to an impossible angle. 'You're with him, then? Where are your parents?'

'They're dead,' said Harry.

'Oh,' Dracina said softly.

Luckily Harry was spared further conversation.

'That's you finished, my dear,' said Madam Malkin quickly, and Harry stepped away so fast that he trod on his own hem.

'Till Hogwarts...' Dracina said quietly. The words seemed to echo after Harry.

Harry did not look round, but he could feel Dracina's eyes needling him as he walked out of the shop.

Outside, Hagrid handed him his ice-cream. It was chocolate and raspberry with twinkling glitter, and he was sure there was something magical about it, but his mind was elsewhere. The Dursleys had only ever bought him the cheapest lemon ice lolly, and only so that he would stop staring at Dudley's Knickerbocker Glory. He ought to have been enjoying every mouthful.

He ate in silence. He kept replaying the conversation with the pale-faced girl, thinking of his parents and what she had said about Hagrid, who was at that moment humming happily beside him with raspberry sauce in his beard. She might have been the rudest girl he had ever met, and it felt as though those bright, piercing green eyes were still burning into his head.
